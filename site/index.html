1. Abstract

MirtCoin (MIRT) is a Bitcoin-style Proof-of-Work blockchain with a fixed maximum supply of 27,000,000 MIRT. New coins are created only by mining, coins move by digitally signed transactions, and every block is verified again inside each user's own browser.

This paper describes exactly what exists today, what is planned, and what the risks are. Anything not described as live is not live.

2. How the chain works

Blocks. Each block header holds the previous block hash, a merkle root of the transactions, a timestamp, the difficulty in bits and a nonce. The header is hashed with double SHA-256, the same hash construction Bitcoin uses.

Mining. A block is valid only if its hash begins with at least the required number of zero bits. Miners change the nonce until they find such a hash. The first valid block extends the chain and pays the block reward to the miner.

Transactions. MirtCoin uses the UTXO model like Bitcoin. A transaction spends earlier outputs and creates new ones. Each input is signed with secp256k1 ECDSA, the same curve Bitcoin uses, so nobody can spend coins without the private key.

Addresses. Addresses are Base58Check encoded with a checksum and start with the letter M, so a mistyped address is rejected.

3. Supply and emission

Maximum supply: 27,000,000 MIRT. This is enforced by the block rules, not by a promise.

Genesis allocation: 2,250,000 MIRT (8.33 percent of the maximum) exists from block zero. 1,500,000 MIRT is in the creator wallet and 750,000 MIRT is in the reserve wallet. Both addresses are public and visible in the explorer.

Mineable supply: 24,750,000 MIRT. The block reward starts at 7.5 MIRT and halves every 1,650,000 blocks. At the target block time of 2 minutes this is about 5,400 MIRT per day, and the first halving comes after roughly 6.3 years.

4. Difficulty

The chain starts at a difficulty of 24 bits, which needs about 16.8 million hashes on average per block. Every 10 blocks the network compares the real time taken with the target of 120 seconds per block and moves the difficulty by up to 2 bits (a factor of 4). Difficulty never goes below 22 bits.

5. Wallets

New wallets are created from a 24-word recovery phrase. The private key is computed on the user's own device and is never sent to the node. The node only sees signed transactions.

The recovery phrase uses MirtCoin's own word list and checksum. It is not BIP39, so it cannot be imported into other wallets. Anyone who has the 24 words controls the coins, and nobody, including the team, can recover a lost phrase.

The two founder wallets use username and password login from an earlier version. Their funds are meant to be moved to recovery-phrase wallets before the public launch.

6. Network today

Today one official MirtCoin node keeps and serves the shared chain. Browsers download every block from it and check all rules themselves, so the node cannot create coins or spend other people's coins.

It is still one node. If it is offline, nobody can mine or send until it returns, and it could stop relaying someone's transactions. Peer-to-peer syncing between many nodes is the next engineering step and is not live.

7. wMIRT and liquidity

To trade MIRT on a decentralised exchange, a wrapped token called wMIRT can be created on BNB Chain. The rule is simple: the amount of wMIRT in circulation must never be higher than the MIRT held in the public reserve wallet.

The Liquidity section of the website shows the live reserve balance, the wMIRT contract, the trading pool, the USDT paired in the pool and the proof that the pool tokens are locked or burned. Until those rows show real links, there is no DEX trading.

8. Exchanges

No exchange has agreed to list MIRT at the time of writing. The Exchanges section of the website only shows a listing once it is real and has a working link. A listing is never guaranteed.

9. Risks

MIRT may have no value, and you can lose everything you put in. Nothing here is financial advice or a promise of profit.

The software has not had an independent security audit and may contain bugs.

One node means centralisation and availability risk, as explained above.

Losing your 24 words means losing your coins permanently.

Browser mining is slow compared with specialised hardware, so early mining is open to everyone but not efficient.

Laws about crypto assets differ by country and can change. Check the rules where you live before you buy, sell or mine.

10 · FAQ

FAQ

Is this a real public blockchain?

Yes, it is a live chain with Bitcoin-style rules, and your browser verifies every block it downloads. Right now one official node keeps the shared chain. Other nodes cannot sync with it yet, so it is not decentralised yet.

How does mining work?

Your device hashes block headers with double SHA-256 until the result starts with enough zero bits. The first valid hash is a new block and pays the block reward to your wallet.

What is the supply?

27,000,000 MIRT maximum. 1,500,000 sit in the creator wallet and 750,000 in the reserve wallet from block zero. The other 24,750,000 are mined, starting at 7.5 per block and halving every 1,650,000 blocks.

How are wallets secured?

Your private key is computed from your username and password using PBKDF2, then used for secp256k1 signatures, the same curve as Bitcoin. Nothing is stored. Use a long unique password; if you lose it, the wallet cannot be recovered.

Can others send me tokens?

Yes, using your wallet address. Anyone on the mainnet can send MIRT to it.
